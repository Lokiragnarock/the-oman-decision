# Writing and Document Style Guide

Applies to every writing task: concept notes, applications, emails, proposals, reports, outreach. Read this before drafting.

## Language and writing style

- Style: formal, concise, professional and natural.
- Tone: strategic, mature and confident without sounding boastful.
- Voice: clear, direct and human. Prefer active voice.
- Structure: logical headings, short paragraphs and purposeful bullet points.
- Vocabulary: precise business language. Avoid unnecessary jargon and inflated wording.
- Sentences: complete, well connected, each with a clear point.
- Detail: enough context to explain the need, objective and expected support, without overexplaining.
- Formatting: minimal bolding, clean headings, limited bullets, no decorative icons or unnecessary dividers.
- Audience: adapt to the reader and stay professional with senior officials, investors and institutional stakeholders.

## Core writing principles

1. Lead with the objective. State what is needed before explaining the background.
2. Be specific about the ask. Describe support in actionable terms, not vague requests.
3. Provide relevant context. Include existing progress, relationships or timelines that help the reader understand the request.
4. Distinguish current progress from future needs. Make clear what is already under way and where outside support adds value.
5. Use confident, measured language. Show ambition without exaggeration or unsupported claims.
6. Avoid generic corporate jargon. Say "investor introductions", "company incorporation", "strategic partnerships", not "unlocking synergies" or "leveraging ecosystems".
7. Preserve natural phrasing. It should sound like a capable founder writing directly, not a corporate template.

Avoid: "We are looking to leverage the incubation centre's extensive ecosystem to unlock strategic opportunities and facilitate fundraising synergies."

Prefer: "We would appreciate support in connecting with relevant investors and strategic partners as we prepare for our next stage of product development."

Defining principle: substance over presentation. Every sentence communicates a need, gives relevant context, or sets a clear next step.

## Length and density (learned 2026-10-24)

- Lokesh dislikes thick, heavy documents. Draft lean from the start: aim for half the length that feels natural.
- One page when the format allows. Two to three sentences per section, then the ask.
- Never invent facts, names or figures to fill a section. Leave a marked gap instead.

- Small grey footer line. No icons, no decorative dividers, no heavy boxes.
- No em dashes anywhere.
