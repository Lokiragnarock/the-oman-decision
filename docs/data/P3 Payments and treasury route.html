Payments and treasury route: money leaves Oman untaxed, so the constraint on both candidates sits on the Indian side

Oman charges 0% withholding tax on dividends and interest paid to non-residents and has no exchange controls on profit or capital repatriation. The binding rules are India's: outbound financial commitment capped at 400% of the investor's net worth, and a 90-day repatriation clock. For ACME the open question is currency and borrower of the REC loan. For OMIFCO the Indian partners no longer sell the urea, so their only recurring cash leg is the dividend.

Compiled 24 Nov 2026 (as-of date for every fetch below unless a source date is given). Sources are numbered [S1] to [S31] and linked in the annexure. Reused from the deck note: company candidates, CEPA dates, CBO and RBI repo rates, USD/OMR. Everything else is new.

Five findings

  1. 1. Oman does not tax the way money comes home. A royal directive of 11 January 2023 indefinitely suspended the 10% withholding tax on dividends and interest paid to non-residents, so the effective Oman rate is 0% against a treaty ceiling of 10% or 12.5% on dividends and 10% on interest [S3][S4]. Royalties, technical fees and management fees still carry 10% [S3]. Deloitte states there are no exchange controls on inbound or outbound investment or on repatriation of capital or profits [S3]. The directive text is not public, so the suspension rests on adviser reports. REPORTED
  2. 2. India's ceiling is 400% of net worth, and ACME phases 2 and 3 are the test. Total financial commitment (equity, debt, guarantees) in all foreign entities may not exceed 400% of the Indian entity's net worth on its last audited balance sheet, above which prior approval is needed [S1]. Dues must be repatriated within 90 days of falling due, and the annual performance report is due by 31 December [S2]. Phases 2 and 3 are reported at OMR 1.6bn (USD 4.2bn) [S13]. ACME's net worth was not found in this packet, so headroom is untested. VERIFIED rule GAP net worth
  3. 3. ACME's phase 1 debt is Indian, and the sources disagree on its currency. REC Limited closed about INR 40bn (about USD 488m) on 21 July 2023 [S9]. Zawya instead reports USD 540m of funding against a project of about USD 750m (about 72%, which the article calls 75%), with USD 140m first received, from "international institutions" [S11]. Tenor, rate and borrower entity are not found. No Omani bank is named as a lender to ACME. REPORTED GAP terms
  4. 4. OMIFCO's Indian owners are dividend receivers, not buyers. OQ Trading buys 100% of OMIFCO's exported urea (about 2 mtpa) FOB at Sur, at a 3.0% to 3.5% discount to Middle East FOB netback indices, under an agreement running 1 Aug 2026 to 20 Feb 2031. About 1 mtpa goes on to the Government of India [S16]. IFFCO and KRIBHCO receive OMR dividends: base OMR 71.2m plus special OMR 9.6m for FY2026, then the higher of 90% of net profit or 3% growth for 2027 and 2028 [S14][S15]. The IPO paid OMR 261m to the selling shareholders [S14]. VERIFIED company release
  5. 5. The tax shield and the treaty cover are both less certain than they look. Duqm's 2015 rules give a 30-calendar-year income tax exemption or the lease term, whichever is closer, on licensed zone income [S6]. Law 38/2025 gives 10 years, renewable twice, and lets existing Duqm enterprises keep current privileges until their periods end [S7]. Which regime the June 2026 phase 2 and 3 agreement sits under is not found. The 1997 India-Oman BIT was terminated on 22 March 2017 [S23], and the CEPA text on transfers of funds was not located [S24]. REPORTED GAP

3. Cross-cutting rules

India outbound: Overseas Investment Rules 2022 and RBI Regulations

  • Financial commitment is ODI plus debt other than portfolio investment plus non-fund-based facilities such as guarantees (Rule 2(1)(f)) [S1].
  • Cap: 400% of net worth on the last audited balance sheet (Schedule I para 3(1)); Rule 9(3) lets RBI set aggregate ceilings; above the limit needs prior approval [S1]. Net worth takes the Companies Act 2013 meaning [S1].
  • Performance guarantees count at 50% toward the limit (Reg 5(6)); guarantees cannot be open-ended (Reg 5(3)) [S2].
  • Indian entity lending to a foreign entity: must be ODI-eligible, already hold ODI in the borrower and control it, use a loan agreement with arm's length interest (Reg 3(1), Reg 4) [S2].
  • Reporting: financial commitment at remittance (Reg 10(2)(1)); disinvestment and restructuring within 30 days; annual performance report by 31 December (Reg 10(4)). Repatriation of dues within 90 days (Reg 9(4)) [S2].
  • How REC, as an NBFC lender, is treated when lending abroad: not found in the Master Direction text read.
