Oman's goods surplus is USD 20,111m, but services, primary income and secondary income (mainly remittances) cost USD 21,309m, so the 2025 current account is USD -1,198m

Oman balance of payments line items, 2014 to 2025. Fetched 2026-11-24. Units are USD millions unless stated. Negative means a net outflow or deficit. Tags: 🟢 read directly from the primary or official series, 🟡 reported by a secondary outlet, 🔴 own arithmetic or inference (shown), ⬜ not found.

The goods account and the current account do not contradict each other. Oman earns a large goods surplus every year and then pays out about USD 18.7bn to 22.5bn (USD 27.0bn in 2022) on foreign-delivered services, income due to foreign owners and lenders, and expatriate workers' remittances. The current account is positive only when the goods surplus beats that fixed bill. In 2025 the goods surplus fell to USD 20,111m against a bill of USD 21,309m. 🟢 (computed from World Bank series that reconcile to the published current account within USD 1.4m in every year)

1. Five findings

  1. The goods surplus fell USD 5.8bn in 2025 and the fixed outflow bill did not move, so the current account flipped to a USD 1,198m deficit (-1.1% of GDP). Goods surplus USD 25,930m (2024) to USD 20,111m (2025). Services 7,069m, primary income 4,707m and secondary income 9,533m (all outflows) sum to USD 21,309m, within 4% of 2024's USD 22,107m. The IMF blames softer oil prices (average export price USD 70.6 a barrel). The Central Bank of Oman (CBO) reports hydrocarbon exports down 15% and imports up 2.8% on large investment projects. 🟢 [S1] [S6] [S7] [S3]
  2. Oman needs a goods surplus of roughly USD 19bn to 22bn a year just to break even, so the current account is an oil-price story, not a rising import story. Outside 2022, the combined services, primary income and secondary income deficit ran from USD 18.7bn to USD 22.5bn across 2014 to 2025. The goods surplus was USD 18.1bn in 2018 and 2019 (current account -4.4bn and -4.3bn) and USD 20.1bn in 2025 (-1.2bn). 🔴 (arithmetic on 🟢 data, section 5 outflow-bill table)
  3. Remittances are the largest single leak: USD 9,466m in 2025, equal to 47% of the goods surplus and 8.6% of GDP. The CBO puts workers' remittances at OMR 3,639.6m in 2025, up 3.1% from OMR 3,529.7m. The USD series is flat in a USD 8,118m to 10,991m band since 2014, so it sets a floor under the bill rather than driving 2025. On India's side, Oman supplied 2.5% of India's bank-channel inward remittances in 2023-24 (1.0% in 2016-17, 0.6% in 2020-21), about USD 2.97bn of India's USD 118.7bn. 🟢 / 🔴 [S9] [S10]
  4. The deficit was financed by investment inflows, not by borrowing against reserves: gross FDI inflow was USD 6,747m, down 27.7% from USD 9,331m, and still larger than the deficit. Residents sent USD 4,147m into foreign portfolio assets (CBO: financial sector holding more foreign assets) and reserve assets rose USD 1,042m. The financial account is USD -1,767m against a current account of USD -1,198m; the USD 569m difference is errors and omissions (USD -596m) plus a small capital account (+27m, derived). 🟢 / 🔴 [S2] [S3]
  5. India is on the receiving end of Oman's goods surplus: India's imports from Oman rose 44.8% to USD 6,549m in FY2024-25, leaving India a USD 2,484m deficit. Indian exports to Oman fell 8.2% to USD 4,065m. The India-Oman CEPA (signed 18 December 2025, in force 1 June 2026, per the deck) sits on top of this trade, and part of Oman's outflow to India is the remittance line in finding 3. 🟢 [S11]

Sign convention (BPM6): a negative net entry means net inflow of funds into Oman (liabilities rising faster than assets); a positive reserve change is a rise. FDI net = assets minus liabilities, so FDI net of -6,895 in 2025 equals assets of -148 (a net disinvestment abroad) minus inflows of 6,747. 🟢 FDI inflow, FDI net, portfolio net, reserves, financial account and errors and omissions are World Bank series: BX.KLT.DINV.CD.WD, BM.KLT.DINV.CD.WD, BN.KLT.DINV.CD, BN.KLT.PTXL.CD, BN.RES.INCL.CD, BN.FIN.TOTL.CD, BN.KAC.EOMS.CD. 🔴 Other investment (loans, deposits, trade credit, derivatives) is computed as financial account - FDI net - portfolio net - reserve change because no separate series was found. 🔴 The capital account is computed as financial account - current account - errors and omissions; it comes out small (about +/-30m in 2021 to 2025), which is the test that the three published series are consistent. IMF Table 4 (2024 Article IV) gives capital account 40, 11, -5 for 2021 to 2023, matching the derived 40, 11, -5. [S5]

Conflicts to know. (a) Reserve change: IMF Table 4 shows 3,090 for 2021 against World Bank 3,933; the two sources differ by 843, and "other investment" differs by the same amount in the opposite direction, so the sum is unchanged (5,781). Historical flows in the IMF series include CBO and sovereign-fund accumulation. (b) FDI 2025: Trading Economics shows OMR 2,594m, the World Bank gross inflow of 6,747 at 2.6008 per OMR is OMR 2,594m (same number); the CBO's OMR 2.651bn is the net figure (6,895 / 2.6008 = 2,651). They are one dataset on gross versus net bases. (c) CBO's 2025 report calls the financial account a "net inflow" of OMR 679.4m; the amount equals the World Bank's -1,767 (OMR 679m) which is the BPM6 net-lending sign. Same number, different wording. [S2] [S3] [S4]

8. What drives each account, in plain English

AccountDriverEvidence and link
GoodsOil price times barrels. About two-thirds of goods exports are hydrocarbons, so a 15% fall in hydrocarbon export revenue is worth about USD 6.7bn on 2024's base (🔴 44,510 x 15%), partly offset by non-oil exports; total goods exports fell USD 4.7bn. Imports follow the investment cycle and grew 2.8% in 2025 on big projects.IMF 2025 Article IV: current account turned to a deficit "weighed down by softer oil prices"; average export price USD 70.6; crude and condensate output 1.005m barrels a day. CBO: hydrocarbon exports -15%, oil price -13%. 2023: lower downstream exports after a two-month refinery shutdown cut the surplus by 0.3% of GDP. 🟢 [S1] [S6] [S3] [S5]
2025 hydrocarbon value in USD: not found.
ServicesOman pays out about USD 12.9bn for services and earns USD 5.8bn. The CBO reports the services deficit at OMR 2.718bn, narrowing 1.2%. What is inside the payments (transport and insurance on imports, foreign contractors, travel) is not itemised in the pages read.🟡 CBO via Oman Observer: receipts OMR 2.244bn (+3.5%), payments OMR 4.962bn (+0.9%). Components: not found. [S3]
Primary incomeIncome owed to foreign owners and lenders: dividends, profit shares and interest. Rises with oil profits and with the stock of external debt (USD 62.0bn in 2025). It narrowed 19.3% in 2025 as oil profits fell, and Oman's own investment income receipts rose 63.5%.🟡 CBO via Oman Observer. 🟢 IMF 2025 Article IV Table 4 external debt 62,004. 🔴 That the fall tracks oil profits is inference. [S3] [S1]
Secondary incomeExpatriate wages sent home. Roughly constant because it follows the size of the foreign workforce and their pay, not oil. USD 9,466m in 2025.CBO: +3.1%, tied to economic expansion and labour market conditions (🟡 Oman News Agency). [S8] [S3]
FDILarge energy and industrial projects (the deck's ACME and OMIFCO examples sit here). It is lumpy: high in 2021 (USD 8.7bn gross), 2023 (12.5bn) and 2024 (9.3bn), lower at 6.7bn in 2025.🟢 World Bank series. CBO describes FDI as stable and the financial account as supportive of external stability. 🟡 [S3]
Portfolio and other investmentResidents (banks, sovereign fund) buying foreign assets. Positive portfolio entries in 2022 to 2025 mean Oman is accumulating foreign assets. The IMF notes banks' net foreign assets turned positive at end-2023, the first time since 2014.🟢 IMF 2024 Article IV, External Sector. CBO: 2025 portfolio outflows reflect higher foreign asset holdings of the financial sector. [S5] [S3]
Reserves and pegGross CBO reserves USD 19,304m at end-2025 (IMF), against a 2025 deficit of USD 1,168m, so the deficit equals about 6% of reserves. The IMF projects reserves of USD 17,804m in 2026 on a deficit of USD 3,353m.🟢 IMF 2025 Article IV Table 4. 🔴 6% = 1,168 / 19,304. [S1]

9. What this does for the C2 argument

  • The deficit is cyclical on this evidence: goods surplus below break-even in 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021 and 2025, positive in 2014 and 2022 to 2024. Break-even sits near USD 21bn. 🔴
  • The financing side is steady: reserves rose in 2025 and FDI inflows exceeded the deficit by more than 5 to 1 (6,747 against 1,198). 🔴 for the ratio.
  • Gaps for the next pass: services components, other investment by year, goods exports split for 2014 to 2017 and 2025, the 2026 quarters, bilateral Oman to India remittances. 2026 quarterly BoP: not found (CBO quarterly bulletin blocked; IMF has only an annual projection).
