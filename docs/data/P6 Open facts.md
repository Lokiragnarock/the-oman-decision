# P6 Open facts: ACME Duqm green ammonia

Fetch date for all sources: 2026-11-25.

## 1. Duqm is on the Arabian Sea, outside Hormuz

Status: VERIFIED (location and Hormuz advantage); advantage is qualified by drone strikes.

An Oman Observer opinion piece (29 March 2026, Dr Mythili Kolluru) says Duqm is "facing the Arabian Sea and the Indian Ocean rather than being inside the Strait of Hormuz or the Red Sea". Carnegie (3 March 2026) says it is "outside the Middle East's most volatile maritime chokepoints", with "relative geographic insulation" from regional disruption. Oman Observer (9 March 2026) says Duqm is "located outside the conflict zone" and is promoted as a contingency hub. Maritime Executive (15 November 2026) says Duqm waits averaged 3 days at end-October versus about 9 days at Fujairah.

Caveat for the deck: Duqm is not immune. Search snippets (Kuehne+Nagel, Lloyd's List, Windward) report drone strikes on a Duqm fuel tank in March 2026 and partial port suspensions. I did not open those pages, so treat the strikes as REPORTED only. Carnegie also reports the strike. Oman Observer argues Duqm does not substitute for cargo that starts or ends inside the Gulf.

## 2. Oman government payment delays, 2016 and 2020

Status: 2016 VERIFIED (delay length, no rial total). 2020 REPORTED only, indirect. No total arrears figure found for either year.

2016: The Oman Society of Contractors CEO Shahswar Al Balushi said in April 2016 that settlement delays on state-funded projects were causing serious cash-flow problems spreading to subcontractors and vendors. On 13 November 2016 he said many firms were unpaid for at least six months, and that six months "can be an average". No rial amount was stated. Related: The National (12 August 2017) reports about 2,000 migrant workers unpaid for up to four months after government contract awards to private firms fell over 40%, from RO 3.2bn to RO 1.3bn, as oil fell by more than half from mid-2014.

2020: NOT FOUND as a contractor arrears figure. Only indirect evidence from search summaries, which I did not open: Galfar's H1 2020 results cited more than RO 42mn certified overdue receivables from the government and related entities (Muscat Daily, 29 August 2020, page returned 403 to me), and a finance ministry cut of at least 10% to 2020 spending. Treat as REPORTED at best.

## 3. State support for the Duqm project

Status: land VERIFIED; port, utility, OQ or government equity and Phase 2/3 offtake NOT FOUND.

Land: On 18 May 2025 OPAZ (the Duqm SEZ authority, SEZAD), Hydrom and ACME signed a project development agreement and a land usufruct agreement for Phases 2 and 3, covering 80 sq km, integrating the project into Hydrom's national portfolio. Each phase targets about 71,000 t green hydrogen and 400,000 t green ammonia a year. Phase 1 (100,000 t ammonia a year) is backed by an offtake with Norway's Yara.

June 2026: Hydrocarbon Processing (17 June 2026) reports ACME signed a USD 4.2bn (OMR 1.6bn) investment agreement for Phases 2 and 3, among OPAZ deals in Duqm. It says commercial operations are targeted for 2030 (Phase 2) and 2033 (Phase 3). It states no OQ or government equity, no offtake and no incentives. Land is given as about 10 sq km in that piece and Zawya, versus 80 sq km in the May 2025 OPAZ and Times of Oman reports. This discrepancy is unresolved; cite the OPAZ figure (80 sq km, official) and flag it.

Not found: any OQ or government equity, any state offtake for Phase 2 or 3, any named port or utility subsidy in the pages opened. Carnegie (March 2026) calls Duqm's hydrogen plans "prospective rather than operational at scale" and describes no direct state funding.

## 4. ACME net worth and the ODI cap test

Status: ACME Solar Holdings net worth REPORTED (third-party aggregator, not the filing itself). ACME Group (unlisted parent) net worth NOT FOUND.

Net worth of ACME Solar Holdings, consolidated, 31 March 2026 (FY26, results audited with unmodified opinion per Quartr summary): equity capital INR 121 crore plus reserves INR 4,940 crore = INR 5,061 crore (Screener.in). Prior year, 31 March 2025: 121 + 4,390 = INR 4,511 crore. I could not open the annual report or NSE filing, so the figure is as shown by Screener and may include non-controlling interests.

Test (USD/INR 92.84):
- USD 4.2bn x 92.84 = INR 389,928 million = INR 38,993 crore.
- 400% of INR 5,061 crore = INR 20,244 crore.
- The project is 770% of net worth (38,993 / 5,061), so it exceeds the 400% cap by about INR 18,749 crore. Headroom would cover only USD 2.18bn (20,244 crore / 92.84 = USD 2.18bn), about 52% of the USD 4.2bn.
- Using the Sep 2026 half-year net worth of INR 8,362 crore (Screener, likely unaudited, after a share issue): 400% = INR 33,448 crore, still short of INR 38,993 crore.

Caveats: the cap applies to the Indian entity's financial commitment (equity plus certain guarantees), not necessarily the full project cost, and USD 4.2bn is total investment, much of which may be project debt. Whether the investing entity is ACME Solar Holdings or the unlisted ACME Group is not established. The ODI rule text itself (400% of net worth) was not fetched; it comes from the brief.
