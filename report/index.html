1 Summary and verdict

Modify: lock USD 700/t before phase 2, because at USD 641/t the project loses INR 1,825 crore

ACME should not commit capital to Duqm phases 2 and 3 on today’s terms. At the USD 641/t green ammonia auction price the project earns an IRR of 10.6% in rupees against a hurdle of 11.8% and breaks even near USD 700/t. The verdict is to modify: phase 2 proceeds only once an offtake locks at least USD 700/t, the rial stays unhedged, the rupee remains a natural long-USD position, and phase 3 is tied to an oil trigger. Funding must come largely from project debt and partners, since the plan far exceeds India’s cap on outbound investment.

2 Why ACME is in Duqm

ACME already has USD 750m in Duqm, and phases 2 and 3 add USD 4.2bn, about 4% of Oman’s GDP

ACME is an Indian renewables developer whose first Duqm phase is already financed and contracted. Phase 1 will produce 100,000 t a year of green ammonia for about USD 750m, supported by an INR 40bn (USD 488m) loan from REC Limited in July 202314 and a 100 kt a year offtake with Yara18. In June 2026 ACME signed for phases 2 and 3, reported at OMR 1.6bn (USD 4.2bn), each adding 400 kt a year, with phase 2 targeted for 203015,16. That sum is about 4% of Oman’s USD 109.6bn GDP.

The route for capital is open. The India-Oman CEPA, signed on 18 December 202520, has been in force since 1 June 2026, and Oman levies 0% withholding tax on dividends and interest with no exchange controls19. ACME could instead borrow in dollars or rials against dollar-priced sales, which matches currency and keeps parent equity small. Land is secured through an 80 sq km usufruct agreement, but no state equity or phase 2 and 3 offtake has been found17.

3 The monetary system and the peg

Oman has pegged the rial since 1986, so it imports Fed policy: repo 4.50% against a Fed range of 3.75 to 4.00%

The monetary system has moved from bimetallism to the gold standard, to the dollar-gold order of Bretton Woods in 1944, and since the 1970s to mixed regimes (Exhibit 1)13. A fixed rate gives investors certainty but costs the central bank its policy independence; a flexible rate restores independence and exposes investors to currency risk. The impossible trinity states that a country cannot combine a fixed rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy.

Oman keeps the first two. The rial has been fixed at USD 2.6008 since 19866, or 0.3845 rial per dollar (quoted 0.3850 on 24 November 2026), so policy is imported. The Central Bank of Oman has matched all 15 documented Fed moves since July 2022, setting its repo at the Fed’s upper bound plus 50 basis points7,9: 4.50% against a Fed range of 3.75 to 4.00%8. Futures price a Fed hike on 9 December at about 89 to 95%, which would lift the repo to 4.75%10. India, which floats de jure, set its own repo at 5.50%.

4 Oman’s balance of payments and what it does to ACME

Oman’s current account fell to −1.1% of GDP in 2025, and ACME’s USD 4.2bn equals 63% of a year’s gross FDI

Oman has run a current account deficit in 8 of the 12 years from 2014 to 2025, and the 2025 figure of −1.1% of GDP follows a 2024 surplus of 3.6% (Exhibit 2)2,4. A goods surplus of USD 20.1bn was outweighed by net outflows of USD 7.1bn on services, USD 4.7bn on primary income and USD 9.5bn on secondary income, mostly remittances (Exhibit 3). Hydrocarbon exports fell 15%3, and Oman needs a goods surplus of USD 19bn to 22bn to break even, so the balance is an oil-price story.

The first-order effect falls on Oman’s financing. Gross FDI of USD 6.7bn, down 27.7%, still covered the deficit 5.6 times. ACME’s USD 4.2bn equals 63% of one year’s gross FDI inflow, so the project is part of how Oman funds its deficit, while the deficit rests on the oil price, which also drives the budget: Brent averaged USD 68.3 in 2025 against a fiscal breakeven of USD 57.35.

The second-order effects on ACME run through four channels (Exhibit 4). A tighter budget raises the risk to state support; in 2016 contractors went unpaid for about six months25, though no 2020 figure was found. ACME’s size gives it leverage to lock its Duqm tax regime, since the 2015 rules grant a 30-year exemption21 and Law 38/2025 grants 10 years renewable twice22; which applies to phases 2 and 3 was not found. Reserves of USD 19.3bn1 back the peg and the one-year rial forward trades at −0.03% to +0.07% of spot11, so a rial hedge is nearly free while USD/INR costs 3.4% to 3.5% a year12. The December hike raises ACME’s dollar debt cost but tends to weaken the rupee, lifting the rupee value of dollar cash flows. Duqm lies outside the Strait of Hormuz, though a reported March 2026 strike qualifies that26.

5 Recommendation, limits and what would flip it

Ammonia and Oman’s balance fall together, so the downside is correlated: modify, with phase 2 at USD 700/t

The central risk is that ACME’s revenue and Oman’s capacity to support it share one driver (Exhibit 5). US Gulf ammonia correlates 0.96 with European gas and 0.73 with Brent over 2014 to 202527,28, so an energy-price fall cuts ACME’s offtake price and Oman’s balance together; the downside is correlated, not diversified. The green auction price of USD 641/t is within 1% of Tampa grey ammonia at USD 645/t today29,30 but about 25% above the pre-war USD 515/t. The EU carbon border mechanism, definitive since 1 January 2026 and covering ammonia, is a tailwind31.

5 Recommendation, continued

The team’s model, on stated assumptions, gives an NPV of negative INR 1,825 crore at USD 641/t, an IRR of 10.6% against an 11.8% rupee hurdle, and a break-even near USD 700/t. The ammonia price moves NPV by INR 17,389 crore and the rupee by INR 13,704 crore, far more than the Fed, oil or a peg break (Exhibit 6). The verdict follows: the rial stays unhedged because it moves with the dollar ACME earns, and the rupee exposure is kept as a natural long-USD position.

Funding limits the structure. USD 4.2bn is about INR 38,993 crore, or 770% of the INR 5,061 crore net worth reported for ACME Solar Holdings24, against India’s cap of 400%23. Headroom covers about USD 2.2bn, so equity must be modest and the rest raised as project debt or from partners; the investing entity is not established. Plan B applies if an offtake settles below USD 700/t, Brent stays at or below USD 57, reserves fall below USD 15.0bn or by 20% in a year, or the FDI trend turns. Limits: opex is assumed at 25% of revenue, cost of equity at 12%, the model values phase 2 alone, and outlets differ, for example on land (80 sq km against about 10).
